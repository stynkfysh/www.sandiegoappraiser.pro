Real Estate Appraiser in Escondido, California

Escondido is one of San Diego County's older inland cities, with a historic core of early-20th-century homes surrounding downtown and a broad ring of postwar and late-century subdivisions extending toward the surrounding hills. The city's geography, from valley-floor neighborhoods to hillside citrus and avocado grove parcels, produces one of the more varied housing stocks in North County.

Escondido Real Estate Market

Old Escondido retains a concentration of Craftsman, Spanish Revival, and early ranch homes dating to the 1910s through 1940s, many on larger in-town lots. Surrounding that core, subdivisions built from the 1950s through 1990s make up the bulk of the city's housing supply, while hillside and outlying areas such as Hidden Meadows and Jesmond Dene include custom homes on larger parcels, some with remnant grove acreage. Escondido's proximity to the San Diego Zoo Safari Park and access to Highway 78 and I-15 support steady owner-occupant demand. Because the city spans dense in-town blocks and semi-rural hillside fringe within the same general market area, price per square foot varies widely by specific neighborhood and lot type.

Local Valuation Considerations

Older Old Escondido homes often require adjustment for deferred maintenance, unpermitted additions, and lot configuration relative to newer-construction comparables, while hillside and grove-adjacent parcels in areas like Hidden Meadows may involve private wells, septic systems, or remnant agricultural improvements that affect highest and best use analysis.
